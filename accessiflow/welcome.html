AccessiFlow is installed

It makes websites easier to see, read, hear and use. You do not need to set anything up. Nothing needs to be paid for.

Start here

  1. Find the AccessiFlow button

    Look at the top right of your browser, next to the address bar. The AccessiFlow icon is there. If you cannot see it, click the puzzle piece icon and then click the pin beside AccessiFlow so it stays visible.

  2. Open it and pick what fits you

    The first thing you will see is Quick setup. Pick the one that describes what you need, such as Low vision or Reading support. One click changes the whole page. Nothing is permanent and nothing can break.

  3. Adjust anything you like afterwards

    Below Quick setup there are groups for seeing, reading, moving and clicking, hearing, speaking and more. Every setting says in plain words what it will do. If you cannot find something, type what you want into the search box at the top, such as bigger text.

AccessiFlow remembers your choices for each website on its own. A site you find difficult can stay adjusted without changing anything else.

Smart help, with nothing to set up

Some websites forget to describe their pictures, so a screen reader can only say image. Some fill their pages with links that just say click here. AccessiFlow can repair both.

Open the panel, go to Smart help, and press a button. AccessiFlow writes the missing descriptions, gives the vague links proper names, or explains in a sentence or two what the page is about.

Other tools ask you to sign up for an AI service, copy a long secret code and paste it into a settings box. AccessiFlow does not. That part is already handled, and there is nothing for you to buy or remember.

What gets sent, and when

Smart help is the only part of AccessiFlow that uses the internet, and it only does so when you press one of its buttons. At that moment the picture, or the text of the page, goes to the AccessiFlow helper service, which writes the description and sends it straight back. Nothing is stored afterwards and nothing is linked to you.

Everything else, which is most of what AccessiFlow does, happens on your own computer and never leaves it.

Make AccessiFlow itself comfortable

The panel can be changed to suit you, separately from the websites you visit. Press the circle button at the top right of the panel.

  • Colours. Light, dark, high contrast, or matching whatever your computer already uses.
  • Size of this panel. Makes every word and button in AccessiFlow larger, up to half again.

If you have already told your computer that you prefer less movement or stronger contrast, AccessiFlow follows that from the start. You do not have to ask twice.

Using the keyboard on its own

You never need a mouse. Inside the panel, Tab moves forward, Shift+Tab moves back, Space switches things on and off, and the arrow keys move sliders. These shortcuts work anywhere in the browser:

If something goes wrong

The panel opens but the page does not change.
Browsers do not allow extensions to touch their own pages, such as settings or the extension store. Try an ordinary website. Your choices are still saved either way.
I turned something on and now the page looks wrong.
Open the panel and press Turn everything off at the bottom of Quick setup. The page goes straight back to normal.
Smart help says it is not available.
It needs an internet connection. If you are online and it still says this, the helper service is busy. Everything else keeps working.
A shortcut does nothing.
Another program may have claimed the same keys. You can change them at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.

AccessiFlow follows WCAG 2.2 at levels AA and AAA, and is built to that standard itself. If something here is hard to use, that is a fault worth reporting.
